If you've ever asked a painter for a quote and had no idea whether it was fair, you're not alone. Interior painting pricing in Toronto varies a lot — and most of that variation is legitimate. Here's what actually drives the cost, and what you should expect to pay in 2026.

## The Short Answer

Most Toronto homeowners pay between **$2 and $4 per square foot** for interior painting. For a full house, that typically works out to:

- **1-bedroom condo (600–800 sq ft):** $900 – $1,800
- **3-bedroom semi-detached (1,400 sq ft):** $2,200 – $4,000
- **4-bedroom detached (2,200 sq ft):** $3,500 – $6,500

These are realistic ranges, not minimums. A low quote that seems too good usually means rushed prep work, cheap paint, or an uninsured crew.

## What Drives the Price

### 1. Surface Condition

This is the biggest variable. A new construction or freshly drywalled space is quick to paint — smooth walls, no patching, minimal prep. But older Toronto homes (and there are many) often have:

- Multiple layers of old paint, sometimes with lead
- Hairline cracks from seasonal settling
- Water stains that need shellac primer to block
- Wallpaper that needs to be stripped and the wall skim-coated

Prep work can easily add 20–40% to the job. A painter who quotes without looking at your walls is guessing.

### 2. Paint Quality

The paint itself matters more than most people realize. A quality interior paint like Benjamin Moore Regal Select or Sherwin-Williams Duration costs $70–$90 per gallon. A budget brand from a big box store costs $35–$45.

The difference shows up in coverage (better paints cover in 2 coats vs 3), washability, and how the finish holds up over the years. A professional painter will typically supply paint and factor it into the quote — ask what brand they use.

### 3. Number of Colors and Trim Work

Painting every room a different color slows things down — there's more masking, more cleanup, more chance of error at the edges. Single-color rooms are faster.

Trim (baseboards, door casings, window frames) is also more labour-intensive than walls. Cutting in carefully around all that detail takes time. A full trim repaint usually adds $400–$800 to a house project.

### 4. Ceiling Height

Standard 8-foot ceilings are the baseline. Cathedral ceilings, two-storey foyers, or stairwells require scaffolding or extension poles and extra time. Expect a premium of $200–$500 depending on the area.

### 5. Number of Coats

One coat is rarely enough, especially when:
- Changing from a dark to a light colour
- Painting over bare drywall
- Covering stains

Most quality interior jobs are two coats of finish. If a painter quotes you for one coat, confirm that's really all you need.

## Per-Room Estimates

| Room | Average Cost |
|------|-------------|
| Bedroom (12×12) | $400 – $700 |
| Living room (16×20) | $600 – $1,100 |
| Kitchen (walls only, no cabinets) | $500 – $900 |
| Bathroom | $300 – $500 |
| Hallway/stairwell | $400 – $800 |
| Full basement | $1,200 – $2,500 |

These assume standard 8-foot ceilings, decent wall condition, and two coats of paint.

## What About Labour vs Materials?

On a typical interior paint job, roughly 70–80% of the cost is labour. This reflects:
- Time to prepare surfaces properly
- The skill to cut clean lines without tape (faster and cleaner)
- Professional equipment (spray rigs, scaffolding, drop sheets)
- Cleanup and protection of your furniture and floors

If someone quotes you significantly less than the ranges above, find out what they're cutting. It's usually prep or paint quality — and those shortcuts show up fast.

## Getting a Fair Quote

When comparing quotes from Toronto painters, make sure each one includes:

- Number of coats (walls and ceiling)
- Whether they're supplying paint or you are
- What prep work is included (patching, priming, sanding)
- Paint brand and sheen level
- What's covered if something goes wrong

A legitimate contractor will also be able to show proof of liability insurance and WSIB coverage. Don't skip this — you're liable if an uninsured worker gets hurt in your home.

## When to Paint

Interior painting can be done year-round in Toronto. The one thing that matters is ventilation — especially in winter when you're less likely to open windows. Low-VOC paints help significantly with fumes if you need to stay in the house during the project.

Spring (April–June) is the busiest season for painters, so book early if you want a specific window.
