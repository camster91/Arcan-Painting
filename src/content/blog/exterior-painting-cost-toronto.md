Exterior painting is one of the highest-impact things you can do for a home's curb appeal and long-term durability. It's also more complex than interior work — you're dealing with weather, height, and a surface that takes a beating year-round. Here's what it costs to paint the outside of a Toronto home in 2026.

## Typical Cost Ranges

Exterior painting in Toronto generally runs **$3,000 – $9,000** for a full house, depending on size, siding type, and condition. A more detailed breakdown:

- **Townhouse or semi-detached (1,200 sq ft exterior):** $2,500 – $4,500
- **Detached home (2,000 sq ft exterior):** $4,000 – $7,000
- **Large detached or two-storey (2,800+ sq ft):** $6,000 – $10,000+

These include labour, materials, and prep. Trim, doors, and soffits are typically included in a full exterior quote.

## What Affects the Price

### 1. Siding Material

Different surfaces take paint differently:

- **Aluminum or vinyl siding:** Needs special adhesion primer. Can't just paint over peeling or chalking surfaces.
- **Wood siding or clapboard:** Common in older Toronto homes. Requires scraping, sanding, and possibly caulking gaps before painting.
- **Stucco:** Absorbs more paint and takes longer. Two good coats are essential.
- **Brick:** Usually painted with masonry paint. Harder to reverse if you ever want to strip it back, but very durable when done right.

Wood siding in rough condition (peeling, weathered, some rot) can double the prep time and cost.

### 2. How Much Prep Is Needed

Exterior prep is non-negotiable for a job that lasts. This means:
- Power washing to remove dirt, mildew, and chalking paint
- Scraping and sanding any areas where paint is peeling
- Caulking around windows, doors, and trim
- Priming bare wood or problem areas

A house that's been well-maintained needs maybe 20% of the job time in prep. One that hasn't been painted in 15 years might need 50%.

### 3. Height and Access

Bungalows are faster and safer to paint than two-storey homes. Three-storey or homes with steep rooflines require scaffolding, which adds both time and equipment cost. On a large two-storey home, scaffolding might add $500–$1,500 to the job.

### 4. Number of Colours

Most exterior jobs involve at least two colours: a body colour and a trim colour. Some homes have three or four distinct areas (body, trim, doors, soffits). More colours mean more masking, more care at transitions, and more time.

### 5. Timing and Weather

Exterior painting in Toronto has a real season — roughly **late April through October**. The paint needs dry weather and temperatures consistently above 10°C to cure properly.

This means:
- Booking in advance is important (spring slots fill up fast)
- Rain delays can stretch the timeline
- Summer jobs in July–August often go the smoothest

## What a Good Exterior Paint Job Looks Like

A proper exterior paint job should last **7–12 years** with good prep and quality paint. Signs of a quality job:

- **Power washed and fully dried** before any paint goes on
- **All caulking** checked and replaced where needed
- **Two coats** of a quality exterior paint (Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior or equivalent)
- **Trim masked** or cut in cleanly
- **Full cleanup** including drop sheets, overspray protection

If a painter says they'll skip power washing or only do one coat to save money — that's a red flag. You'll be repainting in 3 years.

## What's Usually Not Included

- Deck or fence painting (quoted separately)
- Window glass cleaning
- Major wood rot repairs (carpentry work is a separate trade)
- Painting detached garage (usually a separate line item)

Ask specifically about what's included. A detailed written quote protects both you and the contractor.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire

1. What exterior paint brand will you use, and what's the warranty on it?
2. Does your quote include power washing and caulking?
3. How many coats are you applying?
4. Are you licensed and insured (liability + WSIB)?
5. What happens if it rains during the job?
