## Why Scarborough Homeowners Have Specific Needs

Scarborough is one of the most diverse parts of Toronto, and that extends to its housing. You'll find post-war split-levels in Agincourt, brick semis in Cliffside, newer townhome developments near Milliken, and everything in between.

Each housing type comes with its own painting challenges. Older homes often have lead paint concerns on trim and window frames — a competent contractor will know to test before sanding. Split-levels with high stairwell walls need the right equipment to reach safely. Newer builds tend to use cheaper builder-grade paint that shows wear faster and may need more coats to cover properly.

A painter who works Scarborough regularly will understand all of this. They won't be surprised by the 14-foot stairwell in your Agincourt split-level or the chalky, faded exterior on your Birchcliff brick semi.

## What to Look for When Hiring a Scarborough Painter

**Insurance — not optional.** Any legitimate painting company should carry both general liability and WSIB (Workplace Safety and Insurance Board) coverage. If a painter falls in your home or damages your property and they're not insured, you could be liable. Ask for proof before signing anything.

**Local reviews.** Google reviews specific to Scarborough or the Toronto east end matter more than a company's overall rating. Look for reviewers mentioning neighbourhoods you recognize — Agincourt, Milliken, Birchcliff, Cliffside, Woburn. A company that's done a dozen jobs in your area is more likely to know what they're dealing with than one driving in from Mississauga for the first time.

**A proper quote process.** Good painters walk your space before quoting. They note the condition of the walls, the prep required, the number of colours, the ceiling height. A quote done over the phone without seeing the job is usually either too low (and will creep up once they start) or too high to compensate for the unknown.

**Clear scope of work.** The quote should specify what's included — surface prep, number of coats, paint brand, cleanup. If it just says "paint living room — $400," that's not enough detail to hold anyone accountable.

## Questions to Ask Before You Hire

**Do you carry WSIB and liability insurance?** If they hesitate, move on.

**Can you walk me through your prep process?** Prep is where most of the quality is determined. A painter who rushes it will leave you with paint that peels within a year.

**What paint brands do you work with?** Benjamin Moore, Sherwin-Williams, and Dulux are the standards in Canada. There's nothing wrong with discussing options — but if they push you toward brands you've never heard of, ask why.

**How long will the job take, and will the same crew be there every day?** Inconsistent crews lead to inconsistent results.

**Do you provide a written warranty?** Reputable companies stand behind their work. Typically a one-to-two year warranty on workmanship is reasonable.

## FAQ

**How much does house painting in Scarborough typically cost?**
Interior painting for an average-sized room generally runs between $300–$600 depending on prep, ceiling height, and the number of coats. Full exterior projects vary significantly based on the size of the home, substrate condition, and how much prep is needed. Get a written quote specific to your home.

**Is there a best time of year to paint in Scarborough?**
Late spring through early fall is ideal for exterior work — temperatures above 10°C are needed for most exterior paints to cure properly. Interior work can be done year-round.

**Can I supply my own paint?**
Most professional painters will work with paint you supply, though some prefer to source their own for warranty purposes. Discuss this upfront.

**How do I know if my older Scarborough home has lead paint?**
Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint on trim and doors. Test kits are available at hardware stores, or ask your painter about assessment. Any sanding of suspected lead paint should follow safe-work practices.
