Choosing an exterior paint color in Canada means thinking about more than aesthetics. The freeze-thaw cycles, UV exposure in summer, and grey winter light all affect how a color looks and performs over time. Here's a practical guide to choosing exterior colors that work in our climate.

## How Canadian Climate Affects Color Choice

### Fade Resistance

Dark, highly saturated colors — deep reds, bright blues, saturated greens — tend to fade faster than neutrals and muted tones. The UV exposure in Canadian summers, combined with the temperature swings, accelerates this. A rich burgundy that looks stunning on day one can look washed out in 3–4 years.

This doesn't mean you can't use dark colors. It means:
- Choose a premium paint with UV-resistant pigments (Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior or Sherwin-Williams Emerald Exterior are the benchmarks)
- Accept that highly saturated colors will need touching up sooner
- Muted or slightly desaturated versions of dark colors hold better than their vivid counterparts

### How Color Reads in Grey Light

Ontario gets a lot of overcast days. Under grey sky, warm colors (creams, warm whites, warm taupes) look more inviting than they do in full sun. Cool colors — blue-greys, slate blues — can feel cold and flat on a cloudy day.

Walk your neighborhood on an overcast day and notice how colors read. The ones that still look warm and welcoming are your best candidates.

### Contrast With Snow

This is something people rarely think about: how does your house look with snow on the ground and trees bare? In winter, a house is the dominant visual element against a white or grey background.

Warm, mid-tone neutrals look grounded and comfortable against snow. Very pale colors can disappear into the background. Very dark colors (charcoal, black) look striking but can show dirt and salt spray from the road.

## Colors That Work Well

### Classic Warm Whites and Creams

The most reliable choice for a Canadian home exterior. Warm whites stay looking clean, work in all light conditions, and have broad appeal.

- **Benjamin Moore White Dove** — a timeless warm white that reads slightly cream in direct sun
- **Sherwin-Williams Alabaster** — works beautifully on trim and body
- **Benjamin Moore Simply White** — brighter and cleaner, good for contemporary homes

### Warm Greys and Greiges

Warm grey (greige — grey + beige) is arguably the most popular exterior color in the GTA right now. It reads as sophisticated without being stark, and holds up well visually in all seasons.

- **Benjamin Moore Revere Pewter (HC-172)** — a warm grey that doesn't read as cold
- **Sherwin-Williams Agreeable Gray (SW 7029)** — slightly more beige than grey; excellent in all light
- **Benjamin Moore Stonington Gray (HC-170)** — a cooler grey that still has enough warmth to work

### Deep Navies and Blues

Navy has become a statement exterior color in Toronto neighbourhoods. It looks excellent on Victorian semis and heritage homes with contrasting white or cream trim.

- **Benjamin Moore Hale Navy (HC-154)** — deep but not too dark; reads as navy even on cloudy days
- **Sherwin-Williams Naval (SW 6244)** — darker and more dramatic; works on contemporary builds
- **Benjamin Moore Newburyport Blue (HC-155)** — a medium blue with grey undertones; more casual than navy

### Earthy and Nature-Inspired

Soft greens, warm browns, and sage tones are gaining traction in Toronto. They complement the brick and stone in many older homes and look great with natural wood elements.

- **Benjamin Moore Tarrytown Green (HC-134)** — muted sage that reads as natural and grounded
- **Sherwin-Williams Sage (SW 0017)** — softer and more neutral; works well on stucco
- **Benjamin Moore Clay Beige (HC-81)** — a warm terracotta-adjacent neutral; excellent on brick homes

## Trim and Accent Colors

Almost every good exterior paint job has at least two colors: body and trim.

**Classic pairings:**
- Warm white or cream body + bright white trim (clean, timeless)
- Navy or deep green body + white or cream trim (bold and traditional)
- Warm grey body + charcoal trim + natural wood door (modern and warm)

**Door colors:** The front door is a place to take a risk. A pop of colour here — rich red, forest green, sunny yellow — can work even on a neutral-coloured house. Benjamin Moore's Heritage Red, Sherwin-Williams Rosemary, or a classic black all photograph beautifully.

## Practical Tips Before You Commit

**Get actual samples, not just chips.** Brush a sample onto the actual siding and look at it in morning light, afternoon sun, and on a cloudy day. Colors shift significantly under different conditions.

**Consider your neighbors.** You don't need to match, but a house that's wildly out of step with the street can affect resale value. Notice what the best-looking houses in your neighborhood have in common.

**Test trim separately.** The body colour and trim colour interact. What looks right on paper might clash on the house. Sample them both and see how they look side by side on the actual building.
