Painting your own home is one of those home improvement projects that looks more manageable than it is. The materials are accessible, YouTube is full of tutorials, and you figure you'll save a few thousand dollars. Sometimes that's the right call. Other times you spend a long weekend on it, end up with a mediocre result, and wish you'd just hired someone.

Here's an honest take.

## Where DIY Interior Painting Works Well

**A single room in good condition.** Painting a bedroom or office with decent walls, no difficult trim work, and a forgiving colour (light over light) is actually pretty approachable for someone willing to do it right. Budget a full day for a 12×12 room — including prep, two coats, and cleanup.

**Touching up existing paint.** If you have leftover paint from a previous job and just need to cover scuffs or nail holes, DIY is a no-brainer.

**When you have the time and are willing to learn.** Cutting in clean lines without tape takes practice. If you enjoy the process and aren't rushing, you can get good results.

## Where Hiring a Pro Makes More Sense

**Multiple rooms or a full house.** A single room might take you a weekend. A full house would take you several weeks of weekends. Most homeowners dramatically underestimate the time involved.

**Old or damaged walls.** Cracks, water stains, wallpaper removal, plaster repair — this is where professionals earn their money. The prep work is hard and requires specific knowledge. Skipping it means the paint looks bad in 6 months.

**High ceilings, stairwells, or complicated spaces.** Working at height with proper equipment is a serious skill. A professional has scaffolding, proper ladders, and experience not falling off them.

**Dark-to-light colour changes.** Painting over dark colours (deep grey, charcoal, burgundy) properly requires the right primer and the right number of coats. Getting this wrong means a blotchy, uneven result that requires additional coats to fix — and each additional coat costs more time.

**If you care about the finish quality.** A professional painter with 10 years of experience cuts cleaner lines, rolls without lap marks, and knows how to work with the paint's open time. This takes practice. The gap between "I painted it" and "a professional painted it" is visible.

## The Real Cost Comparison

Let's be honest about what DIY actually costs:

**Materials for a 3-bedroom house interior:**
- Paint (3–4 gallons per room, 10–12 rooms): $800–$1,200
- Primer: $150–$250
- Rollers, brushes, trays, tape, drop sheets, edger tools: $150–$300
- **Total: $1,100 – $1,750**

**Your time:**
- A homeowner painting a 3-bedroom house, doing it right: 6–10 full days
- If your time is worth $30–$50/hour, that's $1,400–$4,000 in time value

**Professional quote for the same job: $2,500 – $5,000** (depending on prep needed)

So the math is less clear than it looks. You're saving real money only if your time has limited value, you enjoy the work, and you execute it well.

## What Professionals Bring Beyond Speed

- **Liability insurance.** If something goes wrong (a spill on hardwood floors, a crack in the drywall), a professional is accountable. An uninsured contractor — or you — is not.
- **WSIB coverage.** In Ontario, if a worker gets injured in your home, you can be held liable if they're not covered.
- **Better paint.** Professionals typically use better-quality paint than most homeowners buy at retail, and buy it at trade pricing.
- **Warranties.** Most reputable painting companies offer a 1–2 year warranty on their work.

## The Verdict

**DIY makes sense if:** You're painting one room, walls are in good shape, you have time, and you're willing to do proper prep.

**Hire a pro if:** You're doing multiple rooms, walls need work, you want a clean result, or your time is genuinely limited.

There's no shame in either decision. Just be honest about what you're trading — time, quality, and risk — when you make it.
