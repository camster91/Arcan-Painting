Painting a house in Ontario isn't just about picking a color and scheduling a crew. The weather here is genuinely difficult — hot humid summers, cold wet springs, and brutal winters that would crack a fresh paint job before it cures. Timing matters.

## The Basics: Temperature and Humidity

Most exterior paints require:
- **Temperatures above 10°C** (ideally 15–25°C) during application and for at least 24 hours after
- **Relative humidity below 80%** so the paint dries properly
- **No rain** for 24–48 hours after application

Interior painting is much more flexible — you can do it year-round. But exterior painting in Ontario has a real season.

## Season-by-Season Breakdown

### Spring (April – May)
**Rating: Good, with caveats**

Spring is a popular time to book exterior work, and for good reason — the house looks fresh for summer. But early spring in Ontario is unpredictable. A warm week in April can be followed by a week of cold rain.

**Watch for:**
- Late frost (not uncommon in April in Toronto — temperatures can dip near 0°C overnight)
- High humidity and wet conditions from spring rains
- Surfaces that are still cold to the touch even on warm days (north-facing walls especially)

**Best practice:** Wait until late April at minimum, and confirm overnight lows are staying above 10°C before starting exterior work.

### Summer (June – August)
**Rating: Best overall**

This is prime painting season in Ontario. Long days, warm nights, and lower humidity (relative to spring and fall) make for ideal conditions.

**Considerations:**
- Very hot days (35°C+) can cause latex paint to dry too fast and not bond properly. Experienced painters work in the shade or avoid painting in direct sun during peak heat.
- Book early — summer is when most painting companies are fully booked. If you want a July start, book in April or May.
- Humidity in August can be high. Not a dealbreaker, but worth monitoring.

**Interior painting in summer:** Easy. The main thing is ventilation — open windows help paint dry faster and reduce fumes.

### Fall (September – October)
**Rating: Very good for experienced crews**

September and early October are often the best painting months in Ontario. Temperatures are stable, humidity drops, and there's no direct summer sun beating on the walls.

**Watch for:**
- Temperatures dropping off fast in October. By late October, overnight lows in Toronto often dip below 10°C.
- Shorter days mean less working time, especially on large projects.
- Leaves on the ground can blow onto wet paint — not a major issue, just something to manage.

If you're booking fall painting, aim for a September or early October start. Give the crew enough time to finish before it gets cold.

### Winter (November – March)
**Rating: Interior only**

Exterior painting in Ontario winter is a hard no. Even on a warm day in January, temperatures swing wildly and surfaces don't maintain consistent temperatures. You'd end up with adhesion failures, bubbling, and peeling.

**Interior painting in winter:** Totally fine. In fact, winter is often when painters have more availability and scheduling is easier. Just ensure:
- Adequate ventilation (open a window slightly even in cold weather — the paint fumes need somewhere to go)
- The house is heated properly so paint dries at normal speed
- Low-VOC paint if you're staying in the house

## The Practical Takeaway

- **Want exterior painting?** Book for late May through September. Late April and early October can work but are riskier.
- **Need interior painting?** Any time of year works. Winter can actually be a great time to schedule it (less competition, faster booking).
- **Urgent job in cold weather?** Talk to a professional. There are ways to manage marginal conditions, but it requires experience and the right materials.

## A Note on Humidity and Moisture

One thing that trips up exterior paint jobs year-round: moisture in the siding. If it's been raining, you need to wait not just until the surface looks dry, but until it *is* dry — especially with wood siding. A professional painter will check moisture content before starting.

After a heavy rain, plan on waiting at least 24–48 hours before painting — longer for porous materials like stucco or old wood.
