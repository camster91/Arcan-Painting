One of the most common questions we get before starting a project: "How long is this going to take?" It's a fair question — you need to plan around it, especially if you're living in the house.

Here's a realistic breakdown.

## The Short Version

| Project | Typical Duration |
|---------|----------------|
| Single room (bedroom, office) | 1 day |
| 2–3 rooms | 2–3 days |
| Full apartment (1–2 bed condo) | 2–3 days |
| Full house, 3 bedrooms | 4–6 days |
| Full house, 4+ bedrooms with significant prep | 7–10 days |

These assume a two-person crew, standard 8-foot ceilings, and walls in decent condition. Add time for heavy prep, custom finishes, or complex trim work.

## Why It Takes Longer Than You Think

### Prep Can Be Half the Job

On a house that needs significant preparation — patching, priming, sanding, caulking — prep can take as long as the painting itself. A 5-day job might be 2 days of prep and 3 days of painting.

If a painter gives you a timeline that seems very short, ask how much prep is included. A rushed prep job is the main reason paint jobs look bad within a year.

### Dry Time Is Real

Latex paint is dry to the touch in about an hour, but you shouldn't recoat for at least 2–4 hours. Some paints and primer coats need longer. This means:
- Day 1 might be prep and first coat
- Day 2 is second coat and trim

You can't just do everything in a single day and expect a good result.

### Moving Through a Lived-In Home Takes Time

Painters working in an occupied house need to move furniture, protect floors, and work around your life. This adds overhead compared to an empty space. A full house with all your belongings in it takes about 30% longer than the same house empty.

## Room-by-Room Timeline

### Bedroom (12×12 ft, standard ceiling)
- **Walls in good condition, single colour:** 1 day for 2-person crew (prep, prime if needed, 2 coats)
- **Walls needing patching:** Add 3–4 hours
- **Trim included:** Add 3–4 hours

### Living Room / Dining Room (open plan or larger space)
- **Standard condition:** 1–1.5 days
- **High ceilings or feature wall:** Add time accordingly

### Kitchen
- **Walls only (not cabinets):** 1 day
- **Cabinets included:** Add 1–2 days depending on size and finish type

### Bathroom
- **Small bathroom:** Half day
- **Tile surround being painted:** Add 3–4 hours plus proper preparation

### Full Basement
- **Unfinished/utility finish:** 1–2 days
- **Finished basement with trim and doors:** 2–3 days

## What Speeds a Project Up

- **Empty or cleared rooms** (furniture already moved)
- **Walls in good condition** (no major patching required)
- **Single colour throughout** (reduces masking and transitions)
- **Good access** (no tight stairwells, no obstacles)

## What Slows a Project Down

- **Dark colours being painted over with light** (extra prime coats)
- **Textured ceilings or walls** (popcorn ceilings take longer to paint carefully)
- **Lots of trim work** (baseboard, crown moulding, multiple door casings)
- **Old homes with plaster walls** (more cracking, more prep)
- **Multiple colours per room** (more masking and touch-up work)

## Can You Stay in the House?

Usually yes, but plan around it. The main considerations:
- **Fumes:** Low-VOC paints significantly reduce odour. Some fumes are normal for the first few hours; keep windows open and ventilate well.
- **Access:** You'll need to stay out of rooms while they're being worked and while paint dries (a few hours after each coat).
- **Dust from sanding:** Patches get sanded, and that dust travels. Use painter's plastic sheeting to block off areas you don't want affected.

For a full-house repaint, some homeowners prefer to stay with family for a few days. It makes the job go faster and avoids the constant navigation around painters.
